Below is a repository-ready specification. I have treated “near-instant” as an optimisation target to be demonstrated by benchmarks, not as a universal guarantee: classical simulation of general quantum circuits can grow exponentially, so the system must obtain its strongest results from supercompression, symbolic simplification, restricted quantum models, caching, determinisation and parallel execution.

Classical Quantum Supercompression System

GitHub Copilot Prolog Agent Program Requirements

Working name: Quantum-MNN Supercompiler
Suggested repository name: quantum-mnn-supercompiler
Implementation language: SWI-Prolog
Primary deliverable: PROGRAM_REQUIREMENTS.md
Target platform: SWI-Prolog 9 or later on macOS, Linux and Windows/WSL
Project type: Source-to-source compiler, symbolic optimiser, restricted quantum simulator and application showcase

⸻

1. Project Vision

Develop a classical Prolog system that combines:

1. MNN-style manual-neuronet supercompression;
2. Spec to Algorithm, abbreviated S2A;
3. Starlog;
4. Loop2;
5. PLOP;
6. Detlog;
7. Piglog2; and
8. restricted classical simulation of quantum computation.

The system shall accept:

* input/output examples;
* ordinary Prolog programs;
* Starlog expressions;
* MNN rules and tables;
* optimisation declarations;
* quantum circuits;
* hybrid classical–quantum problem descriptions; and
* application-specific constraints.

It shall compile these descriptions into a highly compressed, deterministic and, where profitable, concurrent SWI-Prolog program.

The central design principle is:

Reduce the computation before accelerating its execution.

The system shall seek near-instant responses by combining:

* synthesis from examples;
* specialisation;
* formula discovery;
* structural compression;
* decision-tree compilation;
* removal of unnecessary nondeterminism;
* deterministic loops;
* shared-computation splicing;
* memoisation;
* indexed lookup;
* static concurrency;
* restricted quantum simulation;
* circuit simplification;
* precomputation; and
* reusable compiled solution kernels.

The system shall not claim genuine quantum hardware performance. It shall simulate selected quantum-computing concepts on a classical computer and use those concepts as search, representation and optimisation models.

2. Scientific and Engineering Position

The project shall distinguish three concepts.

2.1 Classical supercompression

Classical supercompression means reducing the size, branching factor, runtime or memory requirements of a computation by discovering a smaller equivalent representation.

Examples include:

* replacing recursion with a closed formula;
* replacing search with an indexed table;
* merging similar clauses into a decision tree;
* compiling repeated outputs into templates;
* calculating shared subgoals once;
* converting choicepoints into finite choice packets;
* reducing a circuit before simulating it;
* specialising a general algorithm for a known domain; and
* compiling frequently asked queries into direct answer paths.

2.2 Classical quantum simulation

The system shall implement selected quantum concepts using ordinary classical data structures and algorithms, including:

* state vectors;
* sparse state vectors;
* stabilizer/tableau simulation;
* reversible Boolean circuits;
* phase and amplitude annotations;
* quantum-inspired search ordering;
* tensor-network-style decomposition;
* circuit rewriting;
* measurement simulation; and
* hybrid symbolic–numeric execution.

2.3 Near-instant computation

“Near-instant” shall be an empirical target for suitable bounded applications.

It shall mean that, after compilation or precomputation, a representative query completes within a configurable response target, such as:

:- qmsc_target_response_time(50).   % milliseconds

The compiler shall report whether the target is:

* statically expected;
* benchmark-confirmed;
* not confirmed;
* impossible under the current representation; or
* dependent on precomputation.

No universal near-instant guarantee shall be made.

3. Source-System Responsibilities

3.1 S2A responsibility

S2A shall provide example-driven algorithm synthesis.

The existing S2A entry point processes named input and output specifications, supports character-breakdown modes, analyses recursive structures and separates examples according to output shape. Its implementation uses input/output records and derives structures from examples before constructing algorithms.

The integrated system shall use S2A to:

* infer candidate algorithms from examples;
* recognise constants and changing values;
* detect recurring structures;
* group examples by input and output shape;
* generate preliminary clauses;
* construct decision trees;
* propose recursive or iterative relations;
* infer symbolic transformations;
* generate candidate quantum-oracle predicates; and
* create training-derived MNN rules.

S2A output shall be treated as an initial candidate rather than automatically trusted final code.

3.2 Starlog responsibility

Starlog shall provide the preferred readable expression language for transformations and pipelines.

Starlog supports result-first expressions of the form Result is function(Args), expands expressions into ordinary Prolog at load time, and provides operators for string concatenation, list append, atom concatenation and term conversion.

The integrated system shall use Starlog for:

* readable generated source;
* nested functional transformations;
* symbolic formula construction;
* circuit-expression construction;
* MNN value pipelines;
* data-preparation pipelines;
* query composition;
* compile-time partial evaluation; and
* representation of preserved symbolic expressions through no_eval/1.

The system shall support at least:

Result is function(Arguments).
String is Left : Right.
List is Left & Right.
Atom is Left • Right.
Term is ..=(Items).
Items is =..(Term).
Result is no_eval(Expression).
Result is eval(Expression).

3.3 Loop2 responsibility

Loop2 shall convert supported finite nondeterministic patterns into deterministic recursion.

Loop2 is designed as a minimal deterministic loop compiler. It eliminates supported findall/3 patterns, flattens nested findall and member, splices simple nondeterministic predicates, converts fact generators into list generators and emits explicit recursive loops.

The integrated system shall use Loop2 to:

* remove supported findall/3;
* compile finite generators into lists;
* turn generator traversal into tail-recursive loops;
* flatten nested enumerations;
* fuse map/filter/collect operations;
* expose loops to later symbolic optimisation;
* remove unnecessary intermediate result lists; and
* prepare deterministic code for Detlog and PLOP.

Loop2 transformations shall preserve, where applicable:

* result contents;
* result order;
* deterministic single success; and
* finite-generator semantics.

3.4 PLOP responsibility

PLOP shall perform whole-program symbolic superoptimisation.

The existing PLOP optimiser includes bottom-up unfolding, memoisation, predicate-to-formula simplification, enumerator analysis, indexical optimisation, subterm-address indexing, recursive index-loop analysis, Gaussian elimination, list-formula discovery, shortcut splicing, shared-subgoal splicing, deterministic loop conversion, safety classification and optimisation reporting.

The integrated system shall use PLOP to:

* unfold small predicates;
* combine chains of transformations;
* eliminate duplicate calls;
* discover closed forms;
* perform algebraic simplification;
* identify recurrent formulas;
* derive direct index access;
* introduce subterm-address indexes;
* splice common subcomputations;
* memoise pure expensive predicates;
* replace general algorithms with specialised kernels;
* simplify generated quantum circuits;
* discover reusable circuit fragments; and
* report every accepted and rejected optimisation.

3.5 Detlog responsibility

Detlog shall make nondeterminism explicit and compile safe cases into deterministic execution.

Detlog currently provides choice packets, splice helpers, loop adapters, explicit decision helpers, source mapping and visible fallback when conversion safety is not established.

The integrated system shall use Detlog to:

* identify hidden choicepoints;
* represent finite alternatives as cp/1;
* combine alternatives through splice operations;
* replace supported cuts with explicit control;
* convert safe nondeterministic predicates;
* preserve fallback paths for uncertain constructs;
* enforce effect ordering;
* generate deterministic intermediate code; and
* verify converted modules for unintended operational cuts.

Required runtime constructs include:

cp(+Alternatives).
member_cp(?Element, +ChoicePacket).
splice(+ChoiceTerms, -Rows).
splice_collect(+ChoiceTerms, -Rows).
splice_each(+ChoiceTerms, :Goal).
splice_first(+ChoiceTerms, -FirstRow).
splice_select(+ChoiceTerms, :Condition, -SelectedRows).
guard(:Goal).
decision(+Alternatives, -Decision).
if_commit(:Condition, :Then, :Else).
first(:Generator, -Result).
once_det(:Goal).
loop_exit(+Reason).

3.6 Piglog2 responsibility

Piglog2 shall insert static concurrency after dependency and cost analysis.

Piglog2 analyses ordinary Prolog source, estimates costs at conversion time, and inserts direct SWI-Prolog concurrency constructs into sufficiently expensive independent sections. Its generated code contains no runtime scheduling policy; decisions are made at conversion time.

Its existing pipeline analyses flattened conjunctions, variable flow and transitive dependencies, estimates costs, finds independent groups and inserts concurrent/3.

The integrated system shall use Piglog2 only after:

* modes have been inferred or declared;
* determinism has been established;
* side effects have been classified;
* shared variables have been analysed;
* quantum-simulation kernels have been selected;
* estimated work exceeds concurrency overhead; and
* memory pressure is acceptable.

Supported declarations shall include:

:- qmsc_cost(PredicateIndicator, Milliseconds).
:- qmsc_long(PredicateIndicator).
:- qmsc_thread_safe(PredicateIndicator).
:- qmsc_thread_unsafe(PredicateIndicator).
:- qmsc_deterministic(PredicateIndicator).
:- qmsc_memory_cost(PredicateIndicator, Bytes).
:- qmsc_parallel_limit(PredicateIndicator, Threads).

4. Integrated Compiler Architecture

The system shall be organised as a sequence of explicit intermediate representations.

4.1 Pipeline

Source or examples
        |
        v
Parsing and source mapping
        |
        v
S2A synthesis
        |
        v
Starlog normalisation
        |
        v
Typed symbolic intermediate representation
        |
        v
MNN supercompression
        |
        v
Loop2 deterministic-loop conversion
        |
        v
PLOP symbolic optimisation
        |
        v
Quantum/circuit optimisation
        |
        v
Detlog determinisation and splice compilation
        |
        v
Piglog2 static concurrency
        |
        v
Verification and differential testing
        |
        v
Generated SWI-Prolog kernel
        |
        v
Benchmark and application showcase

The implementation may repeat optimisation passes until a fixed point is reached.

4.2 Required compiler phases

1. Read source.
2. Parse directives, clauses, examples and quantum terms.
3. Retain source locations.
4. Infer predicate modes.
5. Infer determinism.
6. Identify pure and effectful predicates.
7. Run S2A where specifications are supplied.
8. Expand Starlog into canonical operations.
9. Construct the shared intermediate representation.
10. Build the predicate dependency graph.
11. Build the variable-flow graph.
12. Identify repeated subterms and repeated calls.
13. Build MNN rule tables.
14. Rank compression opportunities.
15. Convert finite nondeterminism into loops or choice packets.
16. Apply PLOP symbolic optimisations.
17. Construct or simplify quantum circuits.
18. Select a classical simulation backend.
19. Apply Detlog conversion.
20. Estimate costs and memory usage.
21. Apply Piglog2 concurrency.
22. Verify semantic obligations.
23. Generate source and reports.
24. Run tests and benchmarks.
25. Record whether the response-time target was achieved.

6. MNN-Style Supercompression

6.1 Definition

For this project, an MNN is a manually inspectable network of:

* rules;
* decision nodes;
* tables;
* formulas;
* invariants;
* indexes;
* constraints;
* shortcuts;
* reusable templates; and
* selected actions.

It shall not require statistical gradient training.

6.2 Compression stages

The compiler shall search for the following, in order:

1. exact constant result;
2. direct lookup table;
3. indexed lookup table;
4. repeated-answer cache;
5. simple arithmetic formula;
6. recurrence relation;
7. finite-state transition;
8. decision tree;
9. shared template with variable slots;
10. common prefix or suffix;
11. subterm-address shortcut;
12. reusable subgoal;
13. reduced search space;
14. bounded choice packet;
15. deterministic loop;
16. reversible circuit;
17. restricted quantum circuit;
18. parallel independent kernels;
19. original algorithm fallback.

6.3 Necessary dependency compression

The compiler shall remove rules, values and branches that do not contribute to requested outputs.

For a query:

query(OutputVariables, Goal)

the analyser shall compute a backward dependency slice from OutputVariables.

Only goals that:

* determine the output;
* constrain a required intermediate value;
* control an effect;
* establish safety; or
* affect success/failure

shall remain in the specialised kernel.

6.4 Hotlist compilation

The system shall support domain hotlists:

:- qmsc_hot_query(
       academy_fee_projection/4,
       frequency(high)
   ).

Frequently used queries may be:

* specialised;
* precomputed;
* memoised;
* compiled into direct decision trees;
* translated into a reduced circuit;
* loaded at startup; and
* benchmarked separately.

6.5 Supercompressed kernel

The final output for a hot query should ideally take one of these forms:

answer(Input, Output) :-
    indexed_answer(Input, Output).
answer(Input, Output) :-
    decision_tree(Input, Output).
answer(Input, Output) :-
    Output is Formula.
answer(Input, Output) :-
    compressed_quantum_kernel(Input, Encoded),
    decode_result(Encoded, Output).

7. Quantum Simulation Requirements

7.1 Supported gate set

The first release shall support:

I, X, Y, Z
H
S, S-dagger
T, T-dagger
RX, RY, RZ
CX/CNOT
CZ
SWAP
Toffoli/CCX
controlled phase
measurement
reset
classical condition

7.2 Simulation backends

The system shall implement at least four backends.

A. Reversible Boolean backend

For circuits composed primarily of:

* X;
* CNOT;
* Toffoli;
* SWAP; and
* classical reversible gates.

Representation:

reversible_state(BitVector).

This backend shall be preferred where phases and superpositions are unnecessary.

B. Stabilizer backend

For Clifford-compatible circuits using gates such as:

* H;
* S;
* CNOT; and
* Pauli gates.

Representation:

stabilizer_tableau(XMatrix, ZMatrix, Phases).

C. Sparse amplitude backend

For circuits where only a small subset of basis states has nonzero amplitude.

Representation:

sparse_state([
    BasisState-Amplitude,
    ...
]).

D. Dense state-vector backend

Representation:

state_vector(QubitCount, Amplitudes).

This backend shall be used only when the estimated state size is below configured limits.

7.3 Optional future backend

A tensor-network backend may be added after the first complete release.

It shall not be required for the minimum viable implementation.

7.4 Backend selection

select_backend(Circuit, Analysis, Backend).

Selection shall consider:

* gate set;
* qubit count;
* entanglement estimate;
* stabilizer compatibility;
* sparsity;
* circuit topology;
* repeated subcircuits;
* required measurements;
* memory limit; and
* target response time.

7.5 Circuit simplification

Required rewrite rules include:

X · X                 → I
H · H                 → I
Z · Z                 → I
S · S                 → Z
T · T                 → S
Gate · Inverse(Gate)  → I
SWAP · SWAP           → I
RZ(a) · RZ(b)         → RZ(a+b)
Adjacent commuting gates may be reordered.
Unmeasured irrelevant qubits may be removed.
Constant-controlled gates may be simplified.
Repeated subcircuits may become named kernels.

7.6 Quantum-inspired optimisation

The system may use quantum concepts without simulating amplitudes.

Supported quantum-inspired methods may include:

* superposition represented as a finite choice packet;
* interference represented as cancellation of symbolic alternatives;
* amplitude represented as a ranking or weight;
* measurement represented as deterministic or pseudo-random selection;
* Grover-inspired candidate ranking;
* annealing-inspired local optimisation;
* reversible constraint propagation; and
* phase-tagged symbolic paths.

These shall be clearly marked as quantum-inspired, not literal quantum simulation.

7.7 Measurement policies

Supported policies:

measurement(exact_distribution).
measurement(most_likely).
measurement(sample(Seed)).
measurement(samples(Count, Seed)).
measurement(symbolic).

Tests shall use seeded sampling when randomness is involved.

8. Hybrid Classical–Quantum Execution

8.1 Hybrid plan

hybrid_plan(
    ClassicalPrefix,
    QuantumKernel,
    ClassicalSuffix,
    Verification
).

The compiler shall:

1. preprocess and compress inputs classically;
2. reduce the active problem size;
3. simulate only the smallest useful quantum kernel;
4. decode results classically;
5. verify constraints;
6. use a classical fallback when required.

8.2 Kernel extraction

The compiler shall identify candidate quantum kernels only where:

* the problem is bounded;
* the data can be compactly encoded;
* a supported circuit can express the operation;
* simulation cost is estimable;
* circuit simplification is useful; and
* the quantum form is competitive with or educationally valuable relative to the classical form.

8.3 Comparative execution

For every showcase, the system shall support:

compare_execution(
    Problem,
    [
        original_prolog,
        compressed_classical,
        quantum_simulated,
        hybrid
    ],
    Report
).

The report shall include:

* compile time;
* precomputation time;
* query time;
* total time;
* memory;
* result correctness;
* generated-code size;
* circuit depth;
* gate count;
* state representation;
* concurrency used; and
* speedup or slowdown.

11. Safety and Semantic Preservation

11.1 Transformation classes

Every transformation shall be classified as:

proved.
verified_bounded.
tested.
claimed.
heuristic.
unsafe.
rejected.

11.2 Fallback

When equivalence is not established, the compiler shall:

* retain the original predicate;
* generate a visible fallback wrapper;
* explain the reason;
* avoid labelling the result as fully converted; and
* continue compiling safe parts where possible.

11.3 Effects

The analyser shall recognise:

* console I/O;
* file I/O;
* network I/O;
* dynamic database changes;
* exceptions;
* random-number generation;
* time;
* sensors;
* actuators;
* global mutable state; and
* foreign predicates.

Effectful operations shall not be:

* duplicated;
* reordered;
* memoised without permission;
* speculatively executed;
* moved across a commit point; or
* executed concurrently unless explicitly safe.

11.4 Quantum simulation limits

The compiler shall reject or fall back when:

* a dense state vector exceeds memory limits;
* an unsupported gate is encountered;
* numeric precision is inadequate;
* symbolic amplitudes grow beyond limits;
* a sampling request lacks a seed in test mode;
* a circuit includes unsupported dynamic behaviour; or
* the estimated runtime exceeds the configured budget.

12. Optimisation Fixed-Point Strategy

The optimiser shall repeat compatible stages until:

* no transformation changes the IR;
* the maximum pass count is reached;
* compile-time budget is exhausted; or
* the cost estimate stops improving.

Suggested pass order:

1. Normalise
2. Specialise
3. Slice dependencies
4. S2A merge
5. Starlog simplify
6. MNN compress
7. Loop2 convert
8. PLOP unfold
9. PLOP formula discovery
10. PLOP index
11. PLOP splice
12. Circuit extract
13. Circuit simplify
14. Detlog convert
15. Dead-code elimination
16. Piglog2 parallelise
17. Verify
18. Repeat where profitable

The system shall prevent optimisation oscillation by hashing each IR state.

16. Benchmark Requirements

16.1 Baselines

Each benchmark shall compare:

1. original Prolog;
2. S2A-generated Prolog;
3. Starlog-normalised code;
4. Loop2 code;
5. PLOP-optimised code;
6. Detlog code;
7. Piglog2 code;
8. simulated quantum implementation;
9. complete hybrid implementation.

16.2 Measurements

Measure:

* wall-clock time;
* CPU time;
* inferences;
* global stack use;
* local stack use;
* trail use;
* allocated bytes;
* generated source size;
* compile time;
* preprocessing time;
* cache construction time;
* cold-query latency;
* warm-query latency;
* throughput;
* quantum gate count;
* circuit depth;
* simulated-state size;
* thread count; and
* correctness.

16.3 Benchmark discipline

* Run warm-up iterations.
* Use multiple measured iterations.
* Report median, mean, minimum and maximum.
* Record machine and SWI-Prolog version.
* Use seeded randomness.
* Prevent dead-code elimination from invalidating tests.
* Verify every result.
* Include workloads where optimisation makes performance worse.
* Do not combine compile time with query time without labelling it.
* Do not describe speedup as quantum speedup when it comes from classical preprocessing.

17. Testing Requirements

17.1 Unit tests

Tests shall cover:

* parsers;
* source mapping;
* S2A adapters;
* Starlog expansion;
* mode inference;
* determinism inference;
* dependency graphs;
* MNN rules;
* Loop2 conversion;
* PLOP transformations;
* choice packets;
* splice operations;
* cut conversion;
* cost estimation;
* concurrency grouping;
* quantum gates;
* backend selection;
* circuit rewriting;
* measurement;
* report generation; and
* fallback behaviour.

17.2 Differential tests

For bounded pure programs:

original_answers(Goal, Answers1),
compiled_answers(Goal, Answers2),
assertion(Answers1 == Answers2).

Where answer order is declared significant, exact order shall be tested.

Where only set equality is promised, the report shall state this explicitly.

17.3 Property tests

Required properties include:

* applying a gate followed by its inverse restores the state;
* simplification preserves measurement distribution;
* deterministic conversion produces at most one top-level success;
* memoisation does not alter pure predicate results;
* concurrency does not introduce shared-variable races;
* removed goals cannot affect outputs;
* compiled lookup answers match source algorithms;
* backend selection respects memory limits; and
* repeated fixed-point optimisation is idempotent.

17.4 Test command

The repository shall provide:

swipl -q -s test/run_tests.pl -g run_tests -t halt

17.5 Benchmark command

swipl -q -s benchmark/run_benchmarks.pl -g run_benchmarks -t halt

17.6 Showcase command

swipl -q -s showcase/run_showcase.pl -g run_showcase -t halt

5. run the full test suite;

7. update documentation;
8. update PROGRESS.md;
9. perform a self-review;
10. record unresolved limitations;

21. Acceptance Criteria

The first complete release is accepted when:

* all required modules exist;
* all six source-system adapters operate or provide explicit dependency diagnostics;
* S2A examples can generate a candidate predicate;
* Starlog expressions can enter the pipeline;
* supported findall/member patterns become deterministic loops;
* PLOP-style symbolic optimisation is demonstrated;
* Detlog choice packets and splice operations work;
* Piglog2 inserts concurrency only into safe independent groups;
* at least four quantum backends are implemented;
* circuit simplification preserves bounded test results;
* the hybrid planner selects between classical and simulated-quantum plans;
* all ten showcases run;
* comparative benchmarks are generated;
* fallbacks are visible;
* every transformation has a provenance record;
* generated code is valid SWI-Prolog;
* the test suite passes; and
* at least one bounded, repeatedly queried showcase reaches its configured warm-query latency target.

22. Non-Goals for the First Release

The first release shall not attempt:

* universal simulation of large quantum computers;
* claims of physical quantum advantage;
* communication with other dimensions or universes;
* arbitrary unbounded quantum circuits;
* full WAM replacement;
* complete preservation of all unrestricted Prolog control behaviour;
* transparent parallelisation of side effects;
* distributed computing;
* GPU kernels;
* real quantum-hardware execution;
* automatic proof of all program equivalences;
* opaque neural-network training; or
* universal near-instant computation.

⸻

23. Final Principle

The completed system shall demonstrate that a classical computer can sometimes approach near-instant application-level responses by compiling a broad computation into a much smaller one.

Its preferred progression shall be:

examples
→ algorithm
→ readable transformations
→ deterministic loops
→ symbolic formulas and indexes
→ compressed choices
→ reduced quantum or quantum-inspired kernel
→ safe concurrency
→ verified specialised answer path

The system’s primary achievement shall not be pretending that a classical machine has become a quantum computer. Its achievement shall be showing how synthesis, symbolic reasoning, manual-neuronet compression, deterministic execution, restricted quantum simulation and static parallelism can cooperate to remove as much unnecessary computation as possible.

This version is suitable as the initial PROGRAM_REQUIREMENTS.md; the most important implementation decision is the shared IR, because it prevents the seven transformation systems from becoming a fragile chain of text converters.
